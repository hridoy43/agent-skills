# Behavior and decision design

Use this reference when message order alone cannot explain or solve the requested behavior. A narrative framework organizes meaning; a behavior model diagnoses whether the audience can, wants to, and is ready to act.

## Framework roles

| Model | Best fit | Diagnostic question | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Elaboration Likelihood Model | High-consideration, risky, technical, skeptical, or regulated decisions | How deeply will this audience examine the argument and evidence? | It is being used to justify weak arguments or superficial authority cues |
| Fogg Behavior Model | A discrete action such as clicking, submitting, purchasing, permitting, or starting | Are motivation, ability, and a timely prompt present together? | The real goal is a continuing behavior shaped by wider capability or environment |
| COM-B | Onboarding, activation, adoption, retention, education, or continuing behavior | Do capability, opportunity, and motivation support the behavior? | A simple message or isolated CTA is the entire task |
| EAST | Generating intervention options | Can the useful behavior become easier, more attractive, appropriately social, or better timed? | It would become a checklist of manipulative nudges or replace direct evidence |
| Decision-friction audit | Existing page, flow, form, checkout, or creative | Are value, relevance, clarity, distraction, anxiety, and genuine timing handled? | The underlying offer, product, or evidence is not yet credible |

These models are optional diagnostics, not alternative story templates. Choose one only when it changes the execution.

## Persuasion depth

Use involvement, risk, reversibility, knowledge, and personal relevance to decide evidence depth:

- **Higher elaboration:** lead with specific meaning, strong arguments, mechanism, comparison, limitations, proof provenance, terms, and risk controls. Weak claims receive more scrutiny and can backfire.
- **Lower elaboration:** make relevance, value, identity, credibility, and the next step quickly understandable. Do not use low attention as permission to hide terms or rely on unsupported cues.
- **Mixed audience:** provide an immediate summary and progressively disclose supporting detail without hiding decision-critical information.

Do not assume that every audience moves through attention, interest, desire, and action in a fixed order. Treat narrative sequences as testable communication structures.

## Diagnose the behavior

For a discrete action, inspect:

1. **Motivation:** Is the outcome relevant and valuable now?
2. **Ability:** Is the action understandable, affordable, accessible, and low enough in effort?
3. **Prompt:** Is the request visible, timely, specific, and predictable?

For continuing behavior, inspect:

1. **Capability:** Does the user have the knowledge, skill, physical access, and confidence?
2. **Opportunity:** Do the environment, timing, device, permissions, social context, and resources support the behavior?
3. **Motivation:** Do reflective goals and immediate feelings support continued use?

Translate the diagnosis into product or content changes. Do not respond to an ability problem with more emotional copy, a capability problem with a louder CTA, or a product-value problem with urgency.

## Ethical decision-friction audit

Check:

- value and relevance to the audience's actual job;
- clarity of offer, action, cost, terms, and consequence;
- unnecessary effort, fields, steps, choices, or cognitive load;
- anxiety caused by missing proof, privacy, security, delivery, cancellation, or recovery information;
- distraction that competes with the primary task;
- timing or urgency that is real, current, approved, and useful;
- accessibility, vulnerability, and unintended pressure.

Reduce accidental friction while preserving informed choice. Never hide material information, obstruct alternatives, preselect harmful defaults, or exploit vulnerability.

## Sources

- Stanford's [Fogg Behavior Model](https://behaviordesign.stanford.edu/resources/fogg-behavior-model) describes motivation, ability, and prompts for behavior.
- The original [Behaviour Change Wheel paper](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3096582/) introduces COM-B after evaluating existing intervention frameworks.
- The [Elaboration Likelihood Model](https://richardepetty.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/1986-advances-pettycacioppo.pdf) explains how involvement and elaboration affect argument and cue processing.
- The Behavioural Insights Team's [EAST framework](https://www.bi.team/publications/east-four-simple-ways-to-apply-behavioural-insights/) is an accessible ideation guide, not a universal law.
- The [LIFT model](https://conversion.com/framework/the-lift-model/) informs the decision-friction audit; use its factors as hypotheses to test in context.
