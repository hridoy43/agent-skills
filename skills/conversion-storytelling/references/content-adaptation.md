# Content and media adaptation

Use this reference only when an approved product story must become a video, thumbnail, social asset, campaign concept, or other content surface. Preserve the same audience, promise, proof, tone, and destination expectation while adapting the expression to the medium.

## Additional brief

Capture only what changes the result:

- platform, placement, organic or paid context, and journey stage;
- format, dimensions or aspect ratio, duration, language, and accessibility needs;
- viewer awareness, expected viewing context, and intended next action;
- available product footage, screenshots, presenter, voice, music, imagery, and brand assets;
- relationship to the title, caption, destination page, product UI, or campaign sequence.

Ask when a missing answer is blocking. For a reversible draft, state assumptions and proceed.

## Adaptation map

| Surface | Narrative job | Required direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Promo video | Show a valuable change and credible mechanism | Hook, promise, problem/progress, demonstration or proof, CTA, duration |
| YouTube or long-form video | Sustain useful attention and deliver the promised depth | Title/thumbnail promise, opening contract, sections, evidence, recap, CTA |
| Short-form video | Communicate one useful idea quickly | Immediate context, one claim, visual proof or example, concise close |
| Thumbnail | Make the topic and stakes legible at small size | One dominant subject, visual hierarchy, minimal text, honest curiosity |
| Static social creative | Stop and orient without losing meaning | One idea, readable hierarchy, accessible contrast, caption relationship |
| Carousel | Teach or persuade through sequence | Cover promise, one step per panel, proof/example, summary, next action |

## Video direction

- Match the opening to the viewer's awareness; do not use an unrelated shock hook.
- Establish what the viewer will learn, see, or be able to do.
- Use demonstration, screen capture, product behavior, attributable evidence, or a concrete example before repeating claims.
- Write for spoken clarity when voiceover is used. Keep on-screen text concise and readable.
- Treat captions, audio independence, pacing, aspect ratio, safe areas, and reduced-motion alternatives as production requirements.
- Place the CTA after enough value and proof for the requested commitment. State the real next step.
- Keep title, thumbnail, opening, body, CTA, and destination page consistent.

## Thumbnail and static direction

- Communicate one dominant promise, tension, comparison, or outcome.
- Use the product, result, person, or object that best represents the actual content.
- Keep text short, distinct from the title when possible, and legible at small size.
- Preserve brand recognition without turning the creative into a logo wall.
- Do not fabricate reactions, interfaces, results, authority, urgency, or product capabilities.
- Specify composition and hierarchy; route image generation, retouching, and export to the relevant production skill.

## Social adaptation

- Repurpose the idea, not identical copy.
- Preserve approved facts while adapting opening, length, structure, visual treatment, and CTA to the platform and placement.
- Use one primary idea and one appropriate action per asset.
- Keep required disclosures, alt text, captions, and important meaning available outside imagery.
- When assets form a sequence, define each asset's job and the progression between them.

## Measurement

Match the metric to the surface and intended behavior. Possible diagnostics include qualified view, completion, retention point, save, share, click, destination engagement, assisted conversion, or content-supported activation. Avoid optimizing an attention metric when it degrades trust, lead quality, destination conversion, or user success.

## Handoff boundary

Return the message strategy, script or beat sheet, visual hierarchy, proof requirements, CTA, accessibility notes, and production constraints. Use `content-marketing-and-brand-growth` for campaign planning and publishing; use the relevant video, image, animation, or design skill for production.
