# Framework selection

## Selection rule

Choose from the audience's awareness, decision risk, evidence, offer complexity, and next action. A framework supplies narrative order; it does not replace product positioning, research, proof, or usability.

Use one primary spine per surface. A secondary pattern may shape a specific section, email, modal, or case study, but do not blend several frameworks until their roles are indistinguishable.

## Framework matrix

| Framework | Best fit | Primary sequence | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| StoryBrand / SB7 | Services, complex offers, trust-building homepages | Customer goal → problem → guide → plan → action → stakes → success | The product experience needs concise instructions rather than a marketing narrative |
| Before-After-Bridge | Compact landing pages, feature launches, transformation sections | Current state → credible better state → mechanism/route | The “after” cannot be supported or the bridge is not differentiated |
| Star-Story-Solution | Case studies and audience-specific customer stories | Real subject → struggle/turning point → solution/outcome | There is no approved, attributable customer evidence |
| Origin story | About, founder, mission, category-creation, or launch narrative | Spark → struggle → breakthrough → buyer relevance | The founder eclipses the customer or the story does not help the purchase decision |
| PAS | Acute, recognized, costly problems | Problem → proportionate consequences → credible relief | It would require shame, fear, invented loss, or exaggerated urgency |
| AIDA | Ads, email, campaigns, and short linear sales paths | Attention → interest → desire → action | A high-consideration buyer needs deeper proof and risk reduction |
| Four Ps | Proof-rich offers and launches | Promise → picture → proof → push | The promise or proof is weak, unapproved, or atypical |
| Jobs-to-be-Done progress | Category switching, onboarding, activation, and retention | Trigger → desired progress → forces/barriers → first value → continuing habit | The task is only a short promotional message |
| Problem-Mechanism-Proof | Technical, regulated, or skeptical B2B buyers | Specific problem → how it works → evidence → risk controls → action | The audience lacks enough category awareness for the mechanism to matter |
| Golden Circle | Brand, mission, category creation, founder-led positioning | Why → how → what → buyer relevance/action | The page needs immediate product clarity or a direct transaction |
| Hero's Journey | Founder stories, customer stories, community narratives | Protagonist → challenge → guides/tools → transformation → invitation | The protagonist is invented, unattributed, or the customer disappears behind the brand |

## Selection questions

1. What does this audience already know, believe, and distrust?
2. Is the next action emotional, evaluative, instructional, or transactional?
3. What proof exists now—not what the team hopes to collect later?
4. How costly or reversible is the decision?
5. Does the user explicitly prefer a framework or tone?

If two frameworks fit, compare them against these questions and recommend one. The user's informed choice wins unless it creates a truth, safety, or legal problem.

## Surface guidance

- **Homepage or service page:** use a complete narrative spine, then proof and objection sections.
- **Product/feature page:** use transformation or problem-mechanism-proof; keep capability details explicit.
- **Case study:** use Star-Story-Solution only with a real subject and approved evidence.
- **Pricing/checkout:** prioritize offer clarity, comparison, terms, risk reduction, and action; avoid decorative story detours.
- **Onboarding:** use progress milestones, action-led language, and immediate feedback. Carry the marketing promise forward, but do not repeat a homepage script.
- **About/origin:** connect founder history to why the customer can trust the product today.
- **Email/ad:** compress one idea and one action; send the user to a page that completes the proof.

## Layering example

For a technical consultancy homepage:

- primary spine: Problem-Mechanism-Proof;
- case-study section: Star-Story-Solution using a real shipped product;
- CTA band: a concise Before-After-Bridge;
- onboarding after booking: a progress checklist, not StoryBrand copy.
