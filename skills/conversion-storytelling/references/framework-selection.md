# Framework selection

Choose by audience awareness, decision risk, available proof, offer complexity, and next action. A framework orders meaning; it doesn't replace positioning, research, proof, or usability. When none adds value, use the approved strategy and healthy conventions.

Roles differ—don't compare them as alternatives: **narrative spine** (orders meaning), **behavior diagnostic** (motivation, ability, prompt, confidence), **execution principle** (hierarchy, scent, disclosure, credibility), **measurement model** (validates the hypothesis).

| Framework | Best fit | Sequence | Avoid when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| StoryBrand / SB7 | Services, complex offers, trust homepages | Goal → problem → guide → plan → action → stakes → success | The surface needs concise instructions |
| Before-After-Bridge | Compact landing, feature launch, transformation | Now → credible better state → route | The "after" is unsupported or the bridge isn't distinct |
| Star-Story-Solution | Case studies | Real subject → turning point → outcome | No approved, attributable customer |
| Origin story | About, founder, mission, launch | Spark → struggle → breakthrough → buyer relevance | Founder eclipses the customer |
| PAS | Acute, recognized, costly problems | Problem → proportionate consequence → relief | It needs shame, fear, or invented urgency |
| AIDA | Ads, email, short linear campaigns | Attention → interest → desire → action | Treated as universal, or buyers need deep proof |
| Four Ps | Proof-rich offers and launches | Promise → picture → proof → push | Promise or proof is weak or atypical |
| Jobs-to-be-Done | Switching, onboarding, activation, retention | Trigger → progress → forces → first value → habit | A short promo message |
| Problem-Mechanism-Proof | Technical, regulated, skeptical B2B | Problem → how it works → evidence → risk controls → action | Audience lacks category awareness |
| Golden Circle | Brand, mission, category, founder positioning | Why → how → what → relevance | The page needs immediate product clarity or a transaction |
| Hero's Journey | Founder, customer, community stories | Protagonist → challenge → guide → transformation → invitation | The hero is invented or the brand replaces the customer |

## Choosing

Ask: what does the audience know and distrust; is the action emotional, evaluative, instructional, or transactional; what proof exists now; how costly or reversible is the decision; does the user prefer a framework; is the real problem message order, confidence, friction, continuing behavior, or execution? If two fit, recommend one; the user's informed choice wins unless it creates a truth, safety, or legal problem. Weight direct product evidence and measured outcomes above framework popularity; no sequence (including AIDA) is a proven universal path.

## By surface

- Homepage or service page: full spine, then proof and objections.
- Product or feature page: transformation or problem-mechanism-proof; capabilities stay explicit.
- Case study: Star-Story-Solution with a real subject and approved evidence only.
- Pricing or checkout: offer clarity, comparison, terms, risk reduction, action—no story detours.
- Onboarding: progress milestones, action-led language, immediate feedback; carry the promise without repeating the homepage.
- About: connect founder history to why the customer can trust the product now.
- Email or ad: one idea, one action, a destination that completes the proof.

Example layering for a technical consultancy: Problem-Mechanism-Proof spine, a Star-Story-Solution case section, a Before-After-Bridge CTA band, and a progress checklist (not StoryBrand copy) after booking.
